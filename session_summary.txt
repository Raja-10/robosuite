11. Confirmed "Nero7" is a real product: AgileX Robotics' "NERO" 7-DOF arm, and its
    gripper is AgileX's "Piper" gripper -- the current arm/gripper collision STLs in
    this repo are already byte-for-byte identical to the official meshes in
    github.com/agilexrobotics/agx_arm_urdf (nero/meshes/*.stl), except
    gripper_flange.stl which differs (untouched, only used for collision).
    However, those STLs are the plain, uncolored COLLISION meshes -- the official
    repo separately ships detailed, per-part-colored VISUAL meshes as .dae
    (Collada) files (nero/meshes/dae/), which is what real AgileX renders use.
    Converted those (via trimesh) into per-material binary STL submeshes (mujoco
    doesn't load .dae) under new vis_meshes/ folders (13MB for the arm, 2.7MB for
    the gripper), and wired them into robot.xml / piper_gripper.xml as separate
    visual geoms per sub-part with real materials/colors (matching Panda's own
    meshes/ vs obj_meshes/<link>_vis/ convention) -- while leaving every existing
    collision geom, joint, and body untouched, so physics/controller behavior is
    unchanged. Along the way: fixed a mujoco "mesh volume too small" error on thin
    decal-like submeshes (added inertia="shell" -- irrelevant for visual-only
    geoms), and discovered gripper_base.dae/gripper_flange.dae are authored in
    millimeters (unlike every other file, which is in meters) -- that ~1000x scale
    mismatch was blowing up the scene's extent and rendering everything else black;
    rescaled those two by 0.001 to fix. Verified pick.py and the controller test
    suite still pass after the swap.
    Also worth noting (not fixed, out of scope for a visual-only request): the arm
    currently has zero collision geometry of its own (all geoms are contype=0/
    conaffinity=0) -- it can't physically collide with the table, objects, or
    itself. Only the gripper has real collision geoms.

12. Found and fixed a real orientation bug: gripper_base's visual sub-meshes (from

    force search over the 24 proper axis-permutation rotations, scored by nearest-
    neighbor distance to the known-correct collision STL (best fit: 0.16mm mean
    residual, i.e. exact). Applied that rotation to all 4 gripper_base_vis
    sub-meshes. Checked every other part (all 8 arm links, gripper_flange,
    gripper_link1/2) the same way -- all were already sub-mm aligned, so this was
    an isolated issue specific to gripper_base's own source file, not a pipeline
    bug. Re-verified pick.py and the controller tests after the fix.

14. Investigated "why doesn't the gripper approach exactly vertically" and did a
    gridded workspace-reachability scan of the table. Root causes, in order found:
      a. Real bug in pick.py: the descend/close/lift go_to() calls never actually
         passed target_mat despite the comment claiming they held orientation --
         so orientation was free to drift after the initial alignment step. Fixed.
      b. Still-bad after (a): target_mat itself was built from whatever orientation
         resulted from the first "move above cube" step, which had NO orientation
         control (already ~28deg off vertical by the time it was captured as the
         "aligned" reference). Fixed by computing target_mat from the orientation
         captured immediately at reset() (~0-2deg off vertical, before any motion)
         instead.
      c. Even after (a)+(b), the "lift" phase alone (moving 0.2m straight up while
         holding the grasped cube) still adds ~20-25deg of tilt on top of a good
         ~6-11deg after approach/descend/grasp. Traced this to joint 6 (the wrist
         joint, actuatorfrcrange +/-8N*m) being torque-saturated 100% of the time
         during that motion -- it's already maxed out just supporting the
         arm+cube's weight in that pose, so no amount of extra commanded torque
         (i.e. no controller gain change) can add more correction there. This is a
         genuine hardware ceiling (matches Nero7's real spec: the wrist motors are
         far weaker than the shoulder/elbow), not a bug. Cube-lift success itself is
         unaffected (height-only check), but the visible tilt during lift is real
         and not further fixable without reducing the lift height/speed or the
         load, or accepting a non-vertical carry.
      Also retuned default_nero7.json's OSC gains as a partial mitigation: kp
      [200,200,200,30,30,30] -> [200,200,200,80,80,80], damping_ratio 2.0 -> 1.5.
      Tested several candidates (Panda's scalar 150/dr1 badly hurt position
      accuracy -- up to 125mm error at a far point -- for better tilt; this one
      trades a smaller, more acceptable amount of position accuracy for a real
      tilt improvement). Verified against the existing controller test suite.
      Separately, did a gridded closed-loop reach scan (holding vertical
      orientation, at grasp height) across the whole 0.76x1.34m table: most of the
      table -- especially right around the base and the far/side edges -- gives
      either large position error or forces significant tilt to reach; only a
      modest zone roughly in front-right of the base (world x in [0.26,0.44],
      y in [0.05,0.15]) tracks both position (<10mm) and verticality (<15deg) well
      simultaneously. Added optional cube_x_range/cube_y_range constructor args to
      Lift (default None, so no other robot/task is affected) and set
      LiftLabSetup1's defaults to a range inside that good zone (offset from
      table_offset: x in [-0.02,0.12], y in [0.09,0.19]).
      Net result verified via 8 fresh pick.py runs with the fixes + new spawn
      region: 7/8 succeeded (the one failure was a near-worst-case ~38deg cube-yaw
      correction); tilt during approach/descend/grasp now runs ~6-14deg (down from
      the original ~24-30deg average), with the lift-phase joint-6-saturation tilt
      noted above still present as a known, hardware-limited residual.

20. Rotated the pick's grasp yaw by 90deg so the gripper doesn't occlude the cube
    from the d435 camera: confirmed the problem first (rendered the pre-fix
    descend pose from d435 -- the cube was fully hidden behind the gripper body),
    added GRIPPER_YAW_OFFSET to pick.py's alignment step (a cube's 4-fold symmetry
    means the perpendicular face pair, 90deg from the "nearest" one, is an equally
    valid flat-face grasp). Adding +90deg broke reliability badly (0/5 picks
    succeeded) -- diagnosed as Nero7's wrist having an asymmetric usable range:
    swinging the wrist to a fixed -90deg target converges to ~10deg residual error,
    but the same magnitude in +90deg only converges to ~50deg. Subtracting the
    offset instead (swinging negative) fixed it: 5/6 picks succeeded after also
    bumping the alignment phase's step budget (80->150, since a ~90-135deg swing
    needs more time than the original <=45deg one). Re-verified the occlusion fix
    still holds with the corrected sign (cube clearly visible next to the gripper
    in the d435 render).

30. Diagnosed "why isn't the motion smooth" by measuring the actual eef speed
    profile through a pick.py run rather than guessing: found the largest jerk
    (3.3 m/s^2 speed change) at the approach->descend phase boundary, because
    go_to's fixed step counts hand off to the next phase's target as soon as the
    budget runs out, whether or not the arm has actually stopped moving (measured
    ~0.2 m/s still at that boundary) -- so the next phase's fresh target abruptly
    redirects existing momentum. Also noted a secondary cause: default_nero7.json
    has interpolation=null, so each phase target is a hard step input into a
    fairly stiff PD controller (kp=200) rather than a smoothed ramp. Not yet
    fixed -- reported the diagnosis; the requested follow-up (below) was the
    home-pose bookend, not this.
